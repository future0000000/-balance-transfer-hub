Balance Transfer Fees: What You're Really Paying

A breakdown of the fees involved in a balance transfer, beyond the advertised interest rate.

The 0% headline rate on a balance transfer card doesn't mean the transfer is free — understanding the full fee picture avoids an unpleasant surprise on your first statement.

The balance transfer fee itself

Typically charged as a percentage of the amount transferred (commonly 3-5%), this fee is added to your new balance immediately, meaning a $5,000 transfer at 3% adds $150 to what you owe from day one.

Calculating whether it's still worth it

Comparing the transfer fee against the interest you'd otherwise pay on the original card during the same period usually shows the transfer still saves money, but running the actual numbers (not just assuming) confirms it for your specific situation.

Fees for other actions

Late payments, exceeding your credit limit, and returned payments typically carry their own separate fees, and a late payment on a balance transfer card can sometimes void the promotional 0% rate entirely, depending on the card's terms.

Annual fees on some balance transfer cards

While many balance-transfer-focused cards have no annual fee, some do — factoring any annual fee into the total cost comparison matters, especially for a transfer you plan to pay off over more than a year.

Reading the terms before applying

The specific fee percentage, minimum fee amount, and promotional period length are all disclosed in a card's terms before you apply — comparing these details directly, not just the marketing headline, gives an accurate cost comparison.
