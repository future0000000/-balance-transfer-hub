Common Balance Transfer Mistakes to Avoid

Frequent mistakes that reduce or eliminate the benefit of a balance transfer strategy.

Balance transfers can genuinely save money on interest, but a few common mistakes frequently undermine the strategy.

Not accounting for the transfer fee upfront

Focusing only on the 0% rate while ignoring the transfer fee can lead to underestimating the true cost, especially for transfers with a higher fee percentage relative to the amount moved.

Missing a payment during the promotional period

On many cards, a single missed or late payment during the 0% period can trigger an immediate switch to the standard (much higher) APR, eliminating the entire benefit of the transfer.

Continuing to use the old card

Transferring a balance but continuing to charge the old card back up defeats the purpose entirely — the debt simply grows again on a second card instead of being paid down.

Underestimating the payoff timeline

Assuming you'll pay off the balance "eventually" without a specific monthly target often leads to a chunk of debt still remaining when the promotional period ends and the standard rate applies.

Applying for too many cards at once

Multiple credit applications in a short period can temporarily lower your credit score and may reduce approval odds — researching and choosing one strong offer tends to work better than applying broadly and comparing after the fact.
