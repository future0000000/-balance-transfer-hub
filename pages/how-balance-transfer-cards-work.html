How Balance Transfer Credit Cards Work

The mechanics of moving debt from one card to another, and what actually happens behind the scenes.

A balance transfer card lets you move existing debt from one or more credit cards onto a new card, typically to take advantage of a lower or 0% introductory interest rate.

The basic process

After approval, you provide the account numbers and amounts for the debts you want to move; the new card issuer pays off those balances directly (up to your credit limit), and the debt now lives on the new card instead.

The introductory period is temporary

The 0% or reduced APR only applies for a limited promotional window, commonly ranging from about 12 to 21 months depending on the card — after that period ends, the standard APR applies to any remaining balance.

A balance transfer fee usually applies

Most cards charge a one-time fee for the transfer itself, commonly around 3-5% of the amount transferred, which is added to your balance immediately even though the interest rate is 0%.

Your old accounts stay open

Transferring a balance doesn't close your old card — it simply pays down that balance. Whether to close the old account afterward is a separate decision with its own credit score implications.

Approval and limits aren't guaranteed

The credit limit on the new card determines how much debt you can actually transfer, and approval depends on creditworthiness — a balance transfer strategy only works if you qualify for a limit large enough to cover the debt you're moving.
