Balance Transfer vs Personal Loan: Which Is Better

Comparing two common strategies for consolidating credit card debt.

Both balance transfer cards and personal loans are common tools for consolidating credit card debt, but they work differently and suit different situations.

How the structure differs

A balance transfer moves debt to a new revolving credit card with a temporary low rate, while a personal loan provides a fixed lump sum with a fixed interest rate and a fixed repayment term from day one.

Predictability of payments

Personal loans offer a fixed monthly payment for a set number of months, which some people find easier to budget around compared to a balance transfer, where the required payment to clear the balance in time depends on self-discipline.

What happens if you don't pay it off in time

A balance transfer's rate jumps to the standard APR after the promotional period if any balance remains, while a personal loan's rate stays fixed for the entire term regardless of how long it takes.

Credit score impact considerations

A balance transfer keeps debt as revolving credit (affecting credit utilization), while a personal loan is installment debt, which is calculated differently in credit scoring models — this can matter depending on your specific credit profile.

Which tends to suit which situation

A balance transfer often works well for debt that can realistically be paid off within the promotional window; a personal loan can make more sense for larger balances needing a longer, more predictable payoff timeline.
